THE STUDY, EXPLAINED

What we tested. What we learned.

The evaluation asked: Does a plan improve when another AI model reviews it—and does that help more than simply asking the original model to revise or review its own work?

It evaluated the quality of written plans. It did not execute those plans or measure whether their proposed projects succeeded.

Three models created plans: Sonnet, Terra, and GLM. Two different models—Astra and Fable—served only as independent judges.

Each author worked on the same six synthetic briefs. For each brief, we compared 11 workflows:

  • The original draft.
  • A plain revision without an adversarial critique.
  • Seven reviewer combinations: each individual model, each two-model pair, and all three together.
  • Two additional controls using two or three independent reviews by the author’s own model.

That produced 33 workflows across six briefs: 198 planned outputs, each intended to receive one Astra judgment and one Fable judgment.

Reviewers received the same original draft. The original author then integrated their anonymous critiques. This helped isolate the reviewer’s contribution while holding the starting plan and final writer constant. Multiple-reviewer workflows were compared with self-review controls using the same number of critics.

The judges assessed requirements, correctness and feasibility, safeguards, sequencing, verification, and clarity. Their rubric scores were expressed on a 0–100 scale. Judgments required supporting citations; invalid judgments remained missing rather than becoming zero scores.

What the results showed

The primary comparison was:

  • Sonnet draft → Terra review → Sonnet revision
  • Sonnet draft → independent Sonnet review → Sonnet revision
Across the five briefs with both outputs available
JudgeAdvantage of Terra review over Sonnet self-reviewDescriptive 95% interval
Astra+9.00 points+3.75 to +13.25
Fable−1.00 point−5.00 to +3.75

Astra saw a meaningful improvement in this sample. Fable did not show a clear advantage. These are paired differences on the same briefs, not differences between averages drawn from different samples.

Other combinations also showed gains. For example, Terra-authored plans reviewed by Terra and GLM scored 84.79 with Astra and 95.83 with Fable. Against the matched two-critic Terra self-review control, that was +2.71 and +4.79 points, respectively. This suggests some combinations were useful, but it does not establish a universally best workflow.

The judges also disagreed sharply about serious problems:

Each flagged judgment contains at least one critical violation
JudgeValid judgmentsJudgments flagging a critical violation
Astra194110
Fable1874

Those flags are model assessments, not confirmed real-world failures. The disparity shows that the conclusion depends substantially on the evaluator. We should not assume either judge is correct simply because it is stricter or more favorable.

How complete and reliable is the evidence?

The study finished with 194/198 plans and 381/396 judgments, using 730/790 authorized calls. One exhausted critique blocked four plans and eight judgments; seven additional Fable judgments failed validation.

The run also required recovery after transport and CLI compatibility problems. Some generation resumed after partial grading and beyond the original deadline. Successful evidence was preserved, and treatment prompts were not tuned against scores, but the result must be described as a recovered, exploratory study.

The practical conclusion is: cross-model review can improve plans, but the benefit varies by workflow and judge. This evaluation does not establish that it reliably beats self-review. Six synthetic briefs and descriptive intervals support identifying promising combinations, rather than broad claims about intelligence or dependable real-world performance.

03 / HOW TO READ THIS

A bounded, exploratory planning screen.

Design. Sonnet (claude-sonnet-5, medium), Terra (gpt-5.6-terra, medium) and GLM (glm-5.3-flash, high) each authored six synthetic briefs. Each author has an original draft, plain revision, all seven nonempty reviewer subsets, and two- and three-critic same-model controls: 11 workflows each, 33 total. Critics independently read the same frozen draft; the original author integrates anonymous critiques. Kimi was excluded after a billing suspension.

Judges. Astra (gpt-6-astra, high) and Fable (claude-fable-5-1, high) judged plans independently, isolated from authorship, previous scores, session history and tools. Six rubric dimensions cover requirements, correctness and feasibility, safeguards, sequencing, verification, and clarity. Citation validation is required. A failed judgment is missing, not a low score.

Coverage. All 33 workflows are represented; four have only five plans. One exhausted Terra critique on the scheduling brief blocked four Sonnet revisions and eight judgments. Seven Fable judgments exhausted output/citation validation. All 18 drafts, 18 plain revisions, 89 critiques and 158 reviewed revisions succeeded. No pending work remains within the frozen attempt policy.

Interpretation. Intervals are unadjusted, descriptive paired bootstrap intervals over at most six briefs. They do not establish confirmatory significance or improved intelligence. Compare paired differences and their sample sizes, rather than subtracting unpaired row means. Multi-reviewer panels use same-model controls matched by critic count.

Recovery. The original screen stopped after a transport error was misclassified. Two excluded recovery attempts exposed CLI metadata/isolation issues; a catalog repair enabled the final recovery. Original artifacts remain unchanged. Generation resumed after partial grading and after the original deadline; this combined cohort is not the original uninterrupted protocol. No score-based selection or treatment-prompt tuning was performed.

Resources. 730/790 authorized stage calls charged, including failed and interrupted attempts; the separate pilot remains 166/200. Known total list-equivalent cost is $66.4590, with four attempts unpriced and 18/33 workflow cost rows incomplete. This is not inferred cash subscription billing. Original run: 2h 19m 12s. Final recovery: 2h 39m 57s. All recovery controllers: 2h 40m 42s. Combined elapsed span: 21h 21m 35s, including the overnight gap.
